## Evidence and limits

The [American Heart Association](https://www.heart.org/en/healthy-living/exercise-and-physical-activity/fitness-basics/warm-up-cool-down) recommends gradually warming up for 5–10 minutes. That general guidance does not establish a precise lifting-load ramp.

A [randomized crossover study of 15 men](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25153744/) found no significant repetition-total or fatigue-index difference between no warm-up, specific, aerobic and combined warm-ups in its tested lifting protocol. This small acute study neither validates this proposed program nor proves that warming up has no value. The exact sets and percentages below are proposed product policy, not research-established optima or injury-prevention guarantees.

## Approved application policy

1. Begin each lifting session with five minutes of easy walking. Count actual elapsed time toward the session budget.
2. Before the first external-load exercise, perform two rehearsal sets: eight reps at no more than 50% of its verified working-load setting, then five reps at no more than 75%. Rest 60 seconds after the first rehearsal and 90 seconds after the second. Keep these comfortably easy; if they are not, do not automatically advance the load.
3. Before each later external-load exercise, perform one rehearsal set of five reps at no more than 50% of its verified working-load setting, followed by 60 seconds of rest. For supersets, rehearse each movement separately before the first working round.
4. Round down to an available equipment setting using the same recorded load convention. These percentages concern the added-plate/displayed-load setting, not effective resistance or 1RM. Never add a guessed machine starting resistance. If there is no verified working load or feasible lighter setting, return `warmup_setup_required` rather than inventing a weight.
5. Bodyweight/assisted movements need their own reviewed rehearsal setup; do not apply ordinary external-load percentages to assistance or invent a new variation. This remains an explicit gap for pull-ups, hanging knee raises and ab-wheel work.
6. Keep warm-up sets separate from working sets and exclude them from progression evidence. Pain/concerning symptoms follow the existing stop policy. Do not prescribe stretching through pain.
7. Preserve all prescribed working sets and rest. Count walking, rehearsal reps, rest and equipment transitions in duration; do not promise a 45–60-minute fit until the timing model is checked. Existing casual stretching is optional personal activity, not a substitute for the defined rehearsal sets.
